Every week a founder tells me they want to "add AI." When I ask what problem it
solves, the room goes quiet. That silence is the real problem.

## A feature is a bet; a strategy is a thesis

Shipping an AI feature without a strategy is shipping a bet you can't reason
about. A strategy answers three questions before you write a line of code:

1. **What expensive human judgment are we trying to scale?**
2. **What does "good enough to trust" look like, measurably?**
3. **What happens when the model is wrong?**

If you can't answer the third one, you're not ready to ship — you're ready to
prototype.

## Start where the data already lives

The fastest wins come from problems where you already have the data and the
feedback loop: support tickets, internal docs, repetitive ops. These are
boring on a pitch deck and transformative in a P&L.

## The takeaway

Strategy isn't bureaucracy at three people. It's the difference between an AI
feature that compounds and a demo that impresses investors and then rots.
