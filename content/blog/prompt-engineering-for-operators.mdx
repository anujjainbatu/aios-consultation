Most "prompt engineering" advice is written for researchers chasing benchmark
points. Operators have a different job: get reliable, reviewable output for a
real workflow. Here's what actually moves the needle.

## Write the contract, not a wish

Vague prompts get vague results. Treat the prompt like an interface:

- State the **role**, the **input**, and the **exact output shape** you expect.
- Show one or two **examples** of good output. Examples beat adjectives.
- Tell it **what to do when it's unsure** — defer, flag, or ask.

## Constrain the output so a human can review it

If the answer comes back as structured fields instead of a paragraph, your team
can review ten of them in the time it takes to read one essay. Reviewability is
a feature.

## Build a "wrongness" check

The operator's superpower isn't a clever prompt — it's a cheap way to catch bad
output before a customer does. Add a verification step: a second pass, a
citation requirement, or a simple rule that rejects obvious nonsense.

## The takeaway

You don't need to out-clever the model. You need to give it a clear contract
and a tight feedback loop. That's an operations problem, and operators are
already good at it.
